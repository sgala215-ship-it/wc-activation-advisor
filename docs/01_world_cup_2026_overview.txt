TOURNAMENT OVERVIEW

The 2026 FIFA World Cup is the first tournament to feature 48 teams (expanded from 32), hosted jointly across the United States, Canada, and Mexico. The tournament runs from June 11 to July 19, 2026.

Host cities in the United States: New York/New Jersey, Los Angeles, Dallas, San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle, Miami, Boston, Kansas City, Atlanta, Houston, Philadelphia.
Host cities in Canada: Toronto, Vancouver.
Host cities in Mexico: Mexico City, Guadalajara, Monterrey.

The final will be held at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey.

SCALE & REACH

The 2026 World Cup is projected to be the most commercially significant sporting event in history:
- Estimated global TV audience: 5+ billion cumulative viewers
- Expected on-site attendance: 5.5 million+ across all matches
- Economic impact projected at $5 billion+ for US host cities alone
- First World Cup on North American soil since USA 1994, which drove a generational spike in soccer participation and MLS founding

NORTH AMERICA SOCCER CONTEXT

Soccer is the fastest-growing major sport in the United States by youth participation and TV ratings. The sport has an estimated 30 million+ registered youth players in the US. MLS has grown from 10 to 30+ clubs since its founding post-1994 World Cup. The US men's national team qualified by right as host nation, giving American fans an automatic rooting interest for the entire tournament.

The Latino/Hispanic demographic is the largest and most passionate soccer fanbase in North America, representing 18% of the US population and disproportionately high soccer consumption. This demographic over-indexes in markets like Los Angeles, Houston, Miami, Dallas, and Chicago — all World Cup host cities.

SPONSORSHIP LANDSCAPE

FIFA's top-tier partners (FIFA Partners) include: Adidas, Coca-Cola, Wanda Group, Hyundai/Kia, Qatar Airways (historically), and several rotating partners. These companies commit to multi-cycle deals worth $100M–$500M+ in total rights fees.

Category exclusivity is a core value driver. A financial services sponsor, for example, can prevent any competing bank from official association with the tournament in all marketing materials.

Rights holders (leagues, clubs, national associations) can run their own activation within World Cup periods provided they do not use official FIFA marks without licensing agreements.

POST-TOURNAMENT COMMERCIAL WINDOW

Historical data from previous World Cup cycles shows:
- New fan registration spikes of 40–120% above baseline during tournament weeks
- App downloads and streaming sign-ups peak during knockout rounds
- Sponsor brand recall is highest in the 30 days immediately following the final
- Season ticket inquiries at domestic clubs spike 25–60% in the 60 days post-tournament
- Merchandise sales remain elevated for 45–90 days after the tournament ends

The post-tournament activation window is widely considered the highest-ROI commercial period in the soccer calendar because:
1. Fan emotional engagement is at peak
2. New audiences are freshly acquired but not yet habituated
3. Competition for media space drops immediately after the final
4. Sponsor relationships are warm and ready for renewal conversations
